What is a DSCR loan?

A DSCR loan is an investment-property mortgage that qualifies you on the property's rent instead of your personal income. No tax returns, W-2s or debt-to-income calculation — the lender asks one question: does the rent cover the mortgage payment? The answer is the debt service coverage ratio. Because the underwriting is property-based, DSCR loans are the workhorse financing for self-employed investors, people with many financed properties, and anyone whose tax returns understate their real cash flow. They usually close in an LLC, come as 30-year fixed, ARM or interest-only products, and price somewhat above conventional investment loans in exchange for the flexibility.

How lenders calculate DSCR

DSCR = Gross monthly rent ÷ Monthly PITIA

PITIA is the full housing payment: Principal, Interest, property Taxes, Insurance and any HOA Association dues. For rent, lenders take the lower of the signed lease and the appraiser's market-rent estimate (the Fannie Mae Form 1007 rent schedule); a vacant property is underwritten on the 1007 alone. A ratio of 1.0 means rent exactly covers the payment; 1.25 means rent is 25% above it.

Lender DSCR vs. investor DSCR — they are not the same number

Investors (and our free ROI calculator) typically compute DSCR as net operating income ÷ annual debt service: rent less vacancy, maintenance, reserves and management, divided by principal and interest only. DSCR lenders instead use gross rent ÷ PITIA — no operating-expense haircut, but taxes, insurance and HOA move into the denominator. On a property with low taxes the lender's number comes out higher; on a high-tax, high-HOA property it can come out lower. Both are useful: the investor version tells you whether you will actually make money, the lender version tells you whether you will get the loan. A worked example

The calculator loads this scenario by default — a $300,000 rental financed at 75% LTV:

Principal & interest (7.0%, 30-yr fixed)$1,496.93
Property tax ($3,600 ÷ 12)$300.00
Insurance ($1,500 ÷ 12)$125.00
HOA$0.00
Monthly PITIA$1,921.93
Gross monthly rent$2,500.00
DSCR = $2,500 ÷ $1,921.931.30

At 1.30 the deal clears the 1.25 top tier with room to spare. Working backward, the rent could support a payment of $2,500 ÷ 1.25 = $2,000, which after taxes and insurance leaves $1,575 for principal and interest — enough to amortize a loan of about $236,700. That is more than the 75%-LTV loan of $225,000, so LTV, not DSCR, is the binding constraint here. Push the rate to 8% and P&I rises to $1,650.97, PITIA to $2,075.97 and the DSCR slips to 1.20 — still above 1.0 but out of the best pricing tier. That sensitivity to rate is why DSCR borrowers often buy points or take an interest-only option on marginal deals.

How the ratio moves your rate. Lenders price in tiers. One representative rate sheet adds roughly 0.1% for ratios between 1.25 and 1.5, 0.3% for 1.0–1.25 and about 0.6% for 0.8–1.0 relative to the best tier, with 1.5+ getting the base price. Credit score and LTV add their own adjustments, so a 1.30 DSCR at 720 credit and 75% LTV will not price like a 1.30 DSCR at 660 and 80%.

Where rates sit. In August 2026, advertised 30-year fixed DSCR rates started around 6.1–6.4% for the strongest files, with most investors landing somewhere between roughly 6.5% and 8% once DSCR, credit and LTV adjustments apply. Rates move daily; enter the quote you actually receive.

Prepayment penalties deserve attention. Unlike conventional loans, most DSCR products carry a penalty for paying off early — commonly a step-down over 3 or 5 years, with shorter or no-penalty options costing a higher rate. If you plan to sell or refinance within that window (for example after a BRRRR rehab), the penalty is a real cost; model it.

Four ways to raise your DSCR

1. Put more down. A smaller loan is a smaller payment. Dropping from 80% to 75% LTV on the example above trims P&I by about $100 a month — often the difference between 1.2 and 1.25. 2. Lower the rate or go interest-only. Buying points or electing an interest-only period (use the payment-type switch above) cuts the payment and lifts the ratio; just remember IO builds no equity and resets higher when amortization begins. 3. Shrink the non-P&I costs. Shop the insurance, check whether the tax assessment can be appealed, and avoid HOA-heavy properties when the ratio is tight — every $50 of monthly carrying cost removed moves the DSCR on a $2,500 rent by roughly 0.03–0.04. 4. Document more rent. A signed market-rate lease, a rent increase at renewal, or furnishing for a higher-rent segment all lift the numerator — but the lender will still cap you at the appraiser's market-rent figure, so the 1007 is the ceiling.

DSCR loans and the BRRRR strategy

DSCR lenders are the most common exit financing for BRRRR deals because they typically require only 3–6 months of seasoning before a cash-out refinance (versus the 6- and 12-month rules on conventional cash-out) and they underwrite on the new rent, not your personal income. The catch is that the cash-out loan is capped by both the LTV limit (commonly 70–75% of the new appraisal) and the DSCR — whichever produces the smaller loan wins. Qualifying is not the same as profiting

A 1.25 DSCR tells the lender the rent covers the mortgage with a 25% margin. It says nothing about vacancy, turnover, the roof, the water heater or the property manager — real operating costs that typically consume 35–50% of gross rent over time. A deal can sail through DSCR underwriting and still lose money.
