What is the BRRRR method?

BRRRR stands for Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. You buy a distressed property well below market value, renovate it, place a tenant, then do a cash-out refinance against the new appraised value. Because the loan is sized on the after-repair value (ARV) rather than what you paid, a well-bought deal returns most — sometimes all — of your invested cash at the refinance, while you keep the property, the tenant and the equity. That recycled cash funds the next deal, which is what makes BRRRR a portfolio-building strategy rather than a single transaction.

The number that matters: cash left in the deal

Cash left in deal = Total project cost − (New loan − Refi closing costs)

Total project cost is everything the deal consumes: purchase price, rehab budget, purchase closing costs, and holding costs while the property is vacant during renovation (if you financed the purchase with a hard-money or private loan, include its points and interest in holding costs — the refinance pays off that loan, and this formula still gives your true cash position). The refinance returns new loan minus closing costs. Whatever remains stuck in the deal is the denominator of your cash-on-cash return — which is why a BRRRR that leaves $5,000 in can show a far higher return than a conventional purchase that leaves $50,000 in, even at identical rents.

The refinance is where BRRRR lives or dies

Two lender constraints decide how much cash you actually get back:

Loan-to-value caps. A conventional (Fannie Mae) cash-out refinance on a 1-unit investment property is limited to 75% LTV (70% for 2–4 units). DSCR lenders — who qualify the loan on the property's rent rather than your personal income — typically offer 70–80% depending on the program, with the more aggressive LTVs demanding stronger credit and coverage ratios.

Seasoning. For a conventional cash-out refi, Fannie Mae requires at least one borrower to have been on title for 6 months, and — since April 2023 — any existing first mortgage being paid off must be at least 12 months old. That 12-month rule matters if you bought with a recorded hard-money first lien. Bought with cash? The delayed-financing exception can let you recover your documented investment without waiting. DSCR lenders are commonly more flexible: 3–6 months of seasoning is typical, and a few programs offer no-seasoning cash-out at reduced LTVs — though within the first year many will cap the appraised value they'll lend against. Model your holding costs on the timeline your lender actually requires, not the timeline you hope for.

The 70% rule: buying right is the whole game

Max offer = 70% × ARV − Rehab budget

The classic flipper's screen works for BRRRR too, and for the same reason: if your all-in cost lands at or under roughly 70–75% of ARV, a refinance at that LTV returns your capital. On the example above, 70% × $260,000 − $40,000 = $142,000 — the $135,000 purchase passes. (Weighing a sale instead of a refinance? Our 70% rule calculator runs the full flip: profit after financing, holding and selling costs, ROI and break-even resale.) Miss on the buy price and no amount of clever refinancing fixes it; the discount you capture at purchase is the cash you extract at refi.

Watch the DSCR — the deal must survive the new mortgage

Pulling maximum cash out means carrying maximum debt. Lenders check the debt service coverage ratio — net operating income divided by the mortgage payment — and DSCR programs commonly want 1.20 or better (our free ROI calculator shows DSCR for any rental, and our DSCR loan calculator computes it the way a DSCR lender does — gross rent ÷ PITIA — along with the maximum refinance loan the rent supports). At today's interest rates, a max-LTV refinance can push an otherwise fine rental to break-even or negative cash flow: the calculator above shows both numbers so you can trade cash-out against cash flow honestly. Many experienced BRRRR investors deliberately refinance below the maximum LTV, leaving a little more equity in to keep the property solidly profitable.

Other ways BRRRR deals go wrong

The appraisal comes in low. Your ARV estimate is a hypothesis; the appraiser's number sets the loan. Base ARV on genuinely comparable renovated sales, and know your numbers at an appraisal 5–10% under your estimate. The rehab runs over. Every extra dollar of renovation is a dollar that stays stuck in the deal — pad the budget and get real bids before closing. The timeline stretches. Holding costs accrue monthly while you renovate, lease and wait out seasoning; a 4-month plan that takes 9 months quietly eats the margin. Estimate expenses honestly — vacancy, maintenance, capital reserves and management (see the expense walk-through in our cap rate guide) — because the refinance lender will.
