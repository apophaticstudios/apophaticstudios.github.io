How this calculator works

The calculator estimates net operating income by taking your monthly rent, subtracting a vacancy allowance, then subtracting operating costs: property tax, insurance, and a combined percentage for maintenance, capital-expenditure reserves, and property management. Subtracting the mortgage payment (principal and interest) gives monthly cash flow.

What the numbers mean

Cap rate is the property's unleveraged yield — annual NOI divided by price — useful for comparing deals regardless of financing (see our dedicated cap rate calculator and guide). Cash-on-cash return measures the yield on the actual cash you put in (down payment and closing costs), which is what most buy-and-hold investors optimize. DSCR (debt service coverage ratio) is NOI divided by the mortgage payment; lenders on investment property commonly require 1.20 or better — see how a lender actually computes it in our DSCR loan calculator. The 1% rule is a fast first-pass screen, not a verdict — pair it with the 50% rule calculator to sanity-check the expense side, or swap the single expense percentage used here for a full line-item budget there.
